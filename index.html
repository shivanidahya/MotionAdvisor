Motions 101

What is a motion?

On this page
The basics

Motions in brief

A motion is a formal request asking a judge to make a specific ruling or order. It is how parties ask the court to act between the complaint and the final judgment, and sometimes after. Because a motion is signed under Rule 11, every factual claim and legal citation in it is the signing attorney’s responsibility, and that is where AI risk lands.

A motion names the relief wanted, gives the legal grounds for it and is supported by facts in the record, usually through affidavits, declarations or exhibits. The other side gets a chance to oppose it in writing, the moving party may reply, and the judge decides with or without a hearing.

Motions can be dispositive, ending all or part of the case (dismissal, summary judgment), or non-dispositive, shaping how the case proceeds (discovery, scheduling, evidence). In Massachusetts Superior Court, most civil motions follow Superior Court Rule 9A: you serve the motion on the other side first, they serve an opposition, and the moving party files the whole package. In federal court in Massachusetts, Local Rule 7.1 requires you to confer with opposing counsel and certify that you did.

About

What is Motion Advisor?

Motion Advisor helps small-firm and solo practitioners decide whether, and how, to use generative AI when drafting a legal motion, and then check the work before it is filed.

What the platform does

  • AI Drafting Advisor. Thirteen multiple-choice questions across six areas (task, confidentiality, competence, verification, jurisdiction and client communication) produce a risk result, a recommended workflow, a verification checklist and the rules to read, downloadable as a PDF.
  • Citation Toolkit. Finds case, statute and rule citations in a pasted or uploaded draft, flags format problems and citations already exposed as AI fabrications, and tracks your verification. Export to Excel, Google Sheets, CSV or PDF.
  • Redaction Game. Three practice rounds on what must come out of a court filing or an AI prompt.
  • Motions 101. What a motion is, its parts, its life from draft to order, and a library of 20 common motions.

How the advisor reaches a result

Each answer carries risk points. Confidentiality and verification count 1.5 times as much as the other areas, and the total becomes a risk index from 0 to 100: 30 or below is “Recommended with safeguards,” 31 to 55 is “Use with limits,” and above 55 is “Not recommended.”

Some answers are stop conditions that force “Not recommended” whatever the score: no citation check, no final review, client information going into a consumer or unknown tool, client information with no data protections, privileged information entered without telling the client, or a court order restricting AI.

Where the legal sources come from

Every explanation traces to a primary source: the ABA Model Rules of Professional Conduct and ABA Formal Opinion 512; the Massachusetts Rules of Professional Conduct and court rules published by the Supreme Judicial Court and Trial Court on mass.gov; the Federal Rules of Civil Procedure; the Local Rules of the U.S. District Court for the District of Massachusetts; and published decisions on AI-fabricated citations.

Each source is listed with what it requires, where the site relies on it, and a link to the official text. Federal and Massachusetts civil-rule citations open on courtrules.net; the official Massachusetts text is published in the Massachusetts Rules of Civil Procedure on Mass.gov ↗.
